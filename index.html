Featured · M.Tech thesis, 2025–26

Keeping platinum clean in formic acid fuel cells

Platinum oxidises formic acid well until carbon monoxide blocks its surface. I modified commercial Pt/C with bismuth and tested a quinone mediator. A very thin bismuth layer helped most; the mediator did not improve peak activity or one-hour retention in these tests.

current retained after a one-hour hold by Bi(1.25)-Pt/C, the catalyst with the least bismuth
92.9%
commercial Pt/C in the same test
58.5%

Retention compares each catalyst with its own current after the first minute, so it measures decay, not output. These are three-electrode half-cell results in acid, with no replicate counts reported. They rank catalysts against each other; full-cell testing is the next step.

Now

Effluent treatment at LOXIM Industries

Since July 2026 I’ve been a Management Trainee in Ahmedabad, leading technology selection for sulphate removal from plant effluent. I evaluated electrocoagulation and electroflocculation as pretreatment and run screening, sample trials and feasibility checks with more than ten vendors.

Shortlisted options are being compared on projected operating cost. The selection is still in progress, so no outcome is claimed here.

Research interests

From half-cells to working devices

The questions I keep coming back to sit where electrochemistry meets process engineering: how a catalyst that looks good in a half-cell behaves in a membrane electrode assembly or a full electrolyser, how it ages over hundreds of hours, and what it takes to run it at plant scale. Electrocatalysis, fuel cells, AEM electrolysis and green hydrogen are where those questions are most alive.

These are interests, not work I have done yet; my device-level experience so far is in three-electrode half-cells.

How the industrial work builds on the lab and design work

  • Technology selection

    Shortlisting effluent-treatment options and screening them for feasibility, the same kind of choice as picking ammonia as the SNCR reagent for performance and cost.

    SNCR design study

  • Electrochemical pretreatment

    Electrocoagulation and electroflocculation use electrodes and an applied current in the effluent, so what I learned about electrodes and electrochemical cells in the lab is relevant to evaluating them.

    Electrode and cell work

  • Vendor trials

    Screening, sample trials and feasibility checks with more than ten technology vendors, reading each claim against what the trial data can actually show.

  • Process economics

    Comparing options on projected operating cost, with the selection still in progress. The SNCR study also costed capital and running costs.

    SNCR economics

  • Safety

    Plant safety induction and HSE training at LOXIM. Earlier, an ignition on a filter reworked a synthesis route at NTNU, and the SNCR design set out ammonia safety measures.
